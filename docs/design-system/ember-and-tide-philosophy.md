# Ember and Tide
### A Personal Design System for Teaching Materials

## The Idea

Ember and Tide is built on a simple duality: two subject worlds, two colours, one shared frame.

On one side sits English B — literary, reflective, narrative. Its colour is **Tide Blue**, a confident, saturated blue rather than a soft or muted one — the colour of depth and distance, fitting for a subject rooted in Greek myth and storytelling.

On the other side sit Information Technology and CAPE Digital Media — hands-on, technical, fast-moving. Their colour is **Ember Orange**, warm and energetic, the colour of sparks and screens and things being built.

Both colours are bright and saturated — closer to true, confident hues than to anything pastel or washed out. They're chosen to feel bold and current, the kind of colour that reads as energetic on a screen a teenager is actually looking forward to opening.

Blue doesn't stay confined to English B, though. A brighter accent blue shows up as a secondary voice inside IT and Digital Media materials too — in icon fills, tags, chart highlights — so blue has real presence across the whole system, not just in one corner of it. Orange still leads the tech identity, but it no longer has the stage entirely to itself.

Beneath both subjects runs the same structural skeleton: the same fonts, the same spacing rhythm, the same rounded, approachable shapes, and the same hand-drawn illustration style. A student moving from an English B handout to an IT slide deck should feel like they're still in the same "world," just visiting a different room in it.

## The Feeling

Clean and minimal, with a hint of playfulness — but confidently colourful rather than quiet about it. Generous white space still gives everything room to breathe, but where colour appears, it appears as a real, saturated presence: a fully filled tag, a solid icon, a bold banner, not just a thin tinted line. That's what makes a system feel exciting to a younger audience rather than well-behaved.

Think: a well-organised, well-lit classroom with genuinely bright posters on the wall, not beige ones.

## The Two Identities

**Tide (English B)**
Calm, literary, a little sophisticated. Headings can lean on a more traditional serif display font for chapter titles and pull quotes — something that feels closer to a well-made book than a textbook. Dark backgrounds, when used, shift into a deep navy tone rather than plain black, keeping the "underwater" feeling of the name.

**Ember (IT & Digital Media)**
Energetic, technical, a little bold. Headings and highlights use a bold, geometric display font — something that feels closer to an app interface or a maker's workshop. Dark backgrounds shift into a deep burnt umber rather than plain black, keeping the "glowing coal" feeling of the name.

Both identities share the same body text font, the same spacing system, the same rounded corners, and the same illustration style — so they read as siblings, not strangers.

## Typography, in Feeling

One quiet sans-serif family carries all the everyday reading — body text, labels, captions — across every subject. It's invisible in the best way: never fighting for attention, just doing its job cleanly.

Headings mostly use that same sans-serif family too, just bolder, so the system feels unified at a glance.

Display moments — chapter openers, big pull quotes, section dividers, the occasional highlighted callout — are where the two identities get to speak in their own voice: serif and literary for Tide, bold and geometric for Ember.

## Illustration and Imagery

Hand-drawn, monochrome line illustrations appear throughout — chapter markers, activity icons, little decorative flourishes — always in a single neutral ink colour regardless of subject. This is deliberate: the illustrations are the one element that stays exactly the same everywhere, a quiet signature that ties every document back to Ember and Tide, no matter which colour or font is leading that page.

Photographs, when used, stay full colour and vibrant — no filters, no duotone treatment. They sit comfortably alongside the more restrained illustrations because they're doing a different job: showing the real world, rather than decorating it.

## Shape and Space

Corners are rounded throughout — buttons, cards, image frames, callout boxes. It's a small thing, but it softens the whole system and keeps it feeling friendly rather than clinical.

Space is generous. Airy layouts, breathing room around text blocks, nothing crammed. Even data-heavy elements like rubrics and quiz breakdowns get room to breathe, while still being allowed a bit of colour and personality rather than sitting there as dry grey tables.

## Backgrounds

Light, neutral backgrounds are the default — this is where body text lives, and where readability matters most. Darker backgrounds are reserved for headings, section breaks, and moments of emphasis, and always shift colour depending on which subject identity is speaking: deep navy for Tide, deep umber for Ember.

## In Short

Ember and Tide is one calm, minimal, well-spaced framework wearing two different jackets — a cool blue, literary jacket for English B, and a warm orange, technical jacket for IT and Digital Media — stitched together by the same fonts, the same rounded shapes, and the same hand-drawn ink illustrations wherever they appear.
