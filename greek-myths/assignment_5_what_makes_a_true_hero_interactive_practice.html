Part B • Section 4 Exam Practice

Strength or Cleverness?

11 Marks
Big Question: During The Twelve Labours of Heracles, what helped Heracles succeed more—his physical strength or his clever thinking?

Clever Thinking Clues: Building a dam to divert a river to clean Lord Augeas’s stables; tricking Atlas into holding the sky again; burning the Hydra’s necks with a hot club so new heads wouldn’t grow.

Physical Strength Clues: Strangling the Nemean lion with his bare hands; carrying the Erymanthian boar; holding up the heavy sky while Atlas fetched the apples.

Model Answers:

Part A (9 marks):
1. Effect (Atlas): Heracles politely asked Atlas to hold the sky for just one moment while he placed a soft pad across his shoulders—and as soon as Atlas took the sky back, Heracles picked up the golden apples and ran off.
2. Effect (King Aegeus): Thinking his son Theseus had been killed by the Minotaur, King Aegeus threw himself off the high cliffs into the sea (which was named the Aegean Sea after him).
3. Moral Lesson: Even brave victories can end in tragedy if we are careless and forget our promises to the people who love us.
