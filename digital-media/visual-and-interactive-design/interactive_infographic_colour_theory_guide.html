The Colour Wheel: Your Map of Colour

Every design system originates from relationships on the wheel.

Click a slice to inspect hue relationships

Primary (3) Secondary (3) Tertiary (6)

1. Primary Colours Red, Yellow, Blue

The origin elements. They cannot be created by mixing any other colors together.

2. Secondary Colours Orange, Green, Violet

Created by mixing equal 50/50 parts of two primaries (e.g., Red + Yellow = Orange).

3. Tertiary Colours Hyphenated Names

Made by mixing a primary with its neighbouring secondary (e.g., Red-Orange, Blue-Green).

The Family Tree Analogy

Primaries are the **grandparents** (the source). Secondaries are their **children**. Tertiaries are the **grandchildren** — a blend of both generations!

Tints, Shades, and Tones: Single Colour Depth

How designers build full interactive UI scale palettes from just one base hue.

Tint

Base Colour + White

Lighter and softer feel. Ideal for disabled buttons, card backgrounds, and subtle badges.

Pink = Red + White

Shade

Base Colour + Black

Darker and heavier feel. Used for hover states, active press states, shadows, and text headings.

Maroon = Red + Black

Tone

Base Colour + Gray

Muted and sophisticated feel. Perfect for background surfaces that shouldn't distract.

Dusty Rose = Red + Gray

Digital Colour Mixing: On-Screen Light

Screens don't use paint — they emit light (RGB Additive Mixing).

Interactive RGB Light Mixing Stage

Adjust Red, Green, and Blue light intensities. Combine all three light beams to produce White!

Additive Mixing (RGB Light)

Unlike paint (subtractive mixing which turns dark brown/muddy), mixing light **adds brightness**. Combining Red + Green + Blue light at full power equals **White**.

Modern Digital Spaces: HSL & OKLCH

HSL (Hue, Saturation, Lightness) mirrors human visual perception. OKLCH is the modern web standard for perceptually uniform color gradients.

CSS dynamic mixing color-mix()

Modern web stylesheets generate tints on the fly using code: 

background: color-mix(in oklch, blue 70%, white);

Colour Psychology & Visual Impact

Colours drive emotion, user focus, and accessibility standards.

Cool Colours

Blues, Greens, Violets

Promote concentration, calm, and stability. Preferred for body interfaces, productivity apps, and dashboards because they minimize eye strain during long screen sessions.

Blue Trust, security, stability.
Green Growth, success, confirmation.

Warm Colours

Reds, Oranges, Yellows

Generate attention, urgency, and arousal. Use deliberately and sparingly (e.g., call-to-action buttons, notifications) to create focal points.

Red Urgency, critical error, passion.
Yellow Optimism, warning highlights.

Accessibility & Inclusive Contrast

1 in 12 people have colour vision deficiency. Never rely on colour alone to convey state!
