01

LINE

The Path of a Moving Point

Direction Weight Emotion

A Line is the most fundamental element of design. It is a mark made by a tool (pencil, brush, vector node) moving across a space. Lines define boundaries, guide the viewer's eye, create structure, and convey distinct psychological moods.

Horizontal Lines

Convey calm, stability, rest, and tranquility (like the horizon).

Vertical Lines

Communicate power, strength, height, and formality.

Diagonal Lines

Express movement, action, tension, and dynamic energy.

Curved Lines

Suggest softness, elegance, organic rhythm, and grace.

SHAPE

Two-Dimensional Enclosed Boundaries

Geometric Organic Abstract

A Shape is a two-dimensional flat area defined by lines, color boundaries, or surrounding values. Shapes have width and height, but no depth.

Geometric Shapes

Precise, mathematical shapes like circles, squares, and triangles. They suggest order, logic, structure, and artificial technology.

Organic (Freeform) Shapes

Irregular, asymmetrical shapes found in nature (clouds, leaves, puddles, human silhouettes). They convey flexibility and natural flow.

03

FORM

Three-Dimensional Depth and Volume

Depth Light & Shadow Volume

Form describes three-dimensional objects with height, width, and depth. In 2D digital art and graphic design, artists create the illusion of form using light source highlights, midtones, core shadows, and cast shadows.

Highlight Where the light source directly strikes the object's surface at full intensity.
Midtone The true natural color of the object, transitioning away from direct light.
Core Shadow The darkest area on the object where light cannot reach.
Cast Shadow The shadow thrown onto the surrounding surface by the object blocking light.

COLOR

Hue, Saturation, Value & Harmonies

Complementary Violet & Amber Psychology

Color is produced when light striking an object is reflected back to the eye. It is characterized by three core properties: Hue (color name like Violet or Amber), Chroma/Saturation (intensity/purity), and Value (lightness vs darkness).

Complementary Scheme Highlight (Violet + Amber)

Colors directly opposite each other on the color wheel (such as Electric Violet and Warm Amber ) create maximum dynamic contrast, vibrant energy, and high visual legibility.

05

VALUE

Lightness or Darkness of a Tone

Grayscale High-Key Low-Key

Value refers to the relative lightness or darkness of a color or tone, spanning from pure white (100% light) to pure black (0% light). High contrast (extreme value differences) creates focus and dramatic emphasis, while low contrast feels subtle and serene.

High-Key Artwork: Dominated by light values (tints) Bright
Low-Key Artwork: Dominated by dark values (shades) Moody

06

TEXTURE

Tactile & Implied Surface Quality

Tactile Visual (Implied) Pattern

Texture refers to the surface quality of an artwork or design—how something feels to the touch (physical/tactile) or how it looks like it would feel (visual/implied).

Physical / Tactile Actual 3D textures you can physically touch (embossed foil paper, thick impasto oil paint, carved wood).
Visual / Implied The 2D illusion of texture created digitally through repeating patterns, noise, grain, and micro-shading.

07

SPACE

Positive/Negative Space & Spatial Illusion

Positive Space Negative Space Perspective

Space refers to the area around, between, or within components of a piece. It can be 2D (flat composition) or 3D (illusion of depth via perspective, overlapping, and size scaling).

Positive Space The primary subject or main graphical focal point in the composition.
Negative Space (White Space) The empty area surrounding the focal point. Crucial for breathing room and visual clarity.
